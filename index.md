Hi, I’m Malcolm, a master’s student in biophysics working on my thesis in complex fluid dynamics at the Max Planck Institute for Dynamics and Self-Organization.

My day-to-day work typically involves a good mix of coffee runs, numerical simulations and pen and paper calculations. 
Currently I'm keen on applying methods from statistical physics to biological systems. In the best case one can start from a microscopic picture and use mean-field theory and informed approximations to arrive at a reasonable mesoscopic model that still captures the essential physics. This allows for a high interpretability of simulation results. In reality many systems are too complex for this approach, which is why I've embraced the use of neural network architectures to study biological systems. Since raw weights rarely tell a story, I focus on designing models so their parameters line up with physical quantities such as rates or coexistence curves, turning a black box into something one can learn from.
